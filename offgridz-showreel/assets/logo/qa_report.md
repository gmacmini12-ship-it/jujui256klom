## Approximate / uncertain (honest list)

- **Circle is not a true circle.** Best true circle RMS = 4.15 px (target < 1 px NOT met); an ellipse gives 1.66 px and the free-form radial curve 0.30 px. The raster layer and `lines.svg` follow the free-form curve, so they sit on the stroke; `geometry.json` reports all three.
- **Stroke width** (3.15 px) is an equivalent width from the un-mixed alpha; the source stroke is soft, so a FWHM would be somewhat smaller.
- **Band opacity fade / y1.** The orange is not a hard-edged rectangle running to the bottom: opacity is ~1 down to the ear, then fades out over the neck. Row where the measured opacity falls below 0.5 / 0.1 (all columns): 972 / 1084; right quarter (x 880-944): 1000 / 1078. The band layer rectangle still extends to the canvas bottom (y1 = 1254) but its alpha is ~0 there (mean alpha of the last 30 rows = 0.01).
- **Band combination law** is estimated, not known. With opacity fixed to 1 on the solid band, RMS residual (8-bit levels) of the best per-pixel photo level: multiply (photo x flat orange x gain K) = 3.1, normal / flat orange over photo (opacity 1) = 7.8, screen (photo screened with orange) = 7.6. Multiply wins clearly; screen and normal blends leave 2.5x and 2.4x more residual. Dark parts of the band stay dark (median RGB [20, 13, 10] over 41776 px), which a normal or screen blend cannot do. With free per-pixel opacity all three models fit about equally (multiply 2.9, normal 3.5, screen 3.5), so that test alone does not discriminate.
- **Gain K = 2.26** (IQR [2.0126860792378407, 2.5868683513525657] over 36 row blocks) assumes the photo is continuous in luminance across the band's left edge (rows 740-930). It only sets the exposure of the un-tinted photo under the band and is the least certain number here. Recomposition does not depend on it (photo and band compensate).
- **Photo under the band** = source divided by the multiply factor per channel (R and G exact; B blended with a warm-grey estimate where the factor is tiny; dark pixels use a warm-grey estimate, so ~5-8 levels of error remain in black areas under the band). Where the opacity was mis-estimated the un-tinted photo shows a slight orange/blue cast. The opacity map is smoothed (sigma 3 px) and filled by normalised convolution in dark areas and under the strip. The band layer continues UNDER the strip with extrapolated opacity (nothing there is visible in the source).
- **Top-right corner of the band** is clipped by the circle line in the source; the layer reproduces what the data show there, not a vector corner.
- **Torn strip edges**: only stretches where bright photo touches the strip are observable (top edge observed on 77% of columns, bottom edge 83%). Elsewhere the strip is black on near-black (hair shadow, far left/right) and its true edge cannot be measured: those stretches are interpolated (gaps < 40 px) or held at the median edge (longer gaps); the left/right ends are vertical cuts 12 px beyond the letters; narrow spikes caused by dark photo areas are removed by a 31 px morphological closing/opening (this also trims genuine narrow torn tips). Photo alpha is 0 inside this silhouette, so the hidden eye area is NOT reconstructed, but the silhouette is only as good as these edges (it may hide a little dark photo, or leave a little strip black in the photo layer).
- **Strip edge ring**: the 1-2 px edge pixels are un-mixed between strip black and the nearest photo colour, which extrapolates the photo by 1-2 px under the strip edge (the only 'invented' photo pixels; PSNR of the 3 px ring around the strip = 44.6 dB).
- **Strip fill under the letters** is Telea inpainting of the strip's black plus deterministic grain; no real content exists there in the source.
- **Clean wordmark**: edge nibbles and speck holes (< 300 px2) are removed, protrusions that survive a 9x9 opening and are thicker than ~2.6 px are cut (the nub at the lower right of the Z), corners are intersections of fitted straight edges, curves are cubic Beziers with 0.7 px tolerance. It is a faithful smoothing of THIS eroded artwork, not a font reconstruction: nominally identical edges can differ by up to ~1 px.
- **Wordmark layer**: edge pixels (1-2 px) are un-mixed against the strip black; the interior keeps the original cream texture and specks at alpha 1. Colour is the source colour, not forced to #EEDFD2.
- **Lines layer**: alpha is un-mixed against the local background; where hair or skin is brighter than ~145 next to the stroke the contrast is too low and alpha falls back to the average stroke profile. Registration marks: top and bottom marks include a short cross bar; left and right marks are plain lines. Endpoints measured at half contrast.
- **Background**: the source has no flat background layer; `layer_photo` carries the near-black everywhere outside the strip, and the recomposition adds #0A0A09 only where alpha is 0.
